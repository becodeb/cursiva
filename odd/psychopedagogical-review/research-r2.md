# R2 — External evidence (graphomotor practice, cursive, game design)

Collected 2026-10-07 with WebSearch only; claims come from search-result snippets, not full-document
reads. Directionally reliable; verify page-level detail before quoting precisely.

## 1. Graphomotor practice at 5–7

- **Stroke progression (Beery VMI order):** vertical → horizontal → circle → cross → square → diagonals → X → triangle (approx. ages 2–3 … 5). Secondary OT summaries, not the Beery manual. https://www.growinghandsonkids.com/free-typical-pre-writing-line-development-handout · https://www.growinghandsonkids.com/pre-writing-skills-checklist-kids.html
- **Direction:** letters formed top→bottom and left→right; one family uses curved strokes. The explicit counter-clockwise rule for c/a/o/d/g was **not** found stated (inference). https://www.dynseo.com/en/graphics-and-writing-supporting-the-child-in-difficulty-occupational-therapy/
- **Tracing vs copying vs memory:** for first-graders, tracing-then-copying was the most effective sequence; for preschoolers it was no better than copying alone. Tracing gave more consistent correct start points than copying from scratch. Verbal description + demonstration beat demonstration alone; self-verbalisation helped further. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00221-018-5319-y · https://lead.ube.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/000425-effects-of-different-types-of-learning-on-handwriting-movements-in-young-children.pdf
- **Multisensory (sand, air-writing, hand-over-hand):** practice consensus, weakly evidenced in what was retrieved.
- **Finger on glass vs pencil, transfer:** bare-finger tablet practice beat stylus on a letter post-test (haptic feedback); tablet-learned tracing transferred to other media. No source found on downsides (e.g. tripod grasp) — that risk is an inference. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01800/full · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5067481 · https://abuhb.nhs.wales/files/childrens-ot/developing-a-tripod-grasp-advice-pdf/

## 2. Cursive

- **Families by shared stroke:** curve a c d g o q; loop b e f h k l; hump m n v w x y z; sometimes a fourth group i j p r s t u. https://www.theottoolbox.com/cursive-letter-families/ · https://mycursive.com/best-order-to-teach-cursive-letters/
- **Joins:** most letters exit low; b, o, v, w exit high and need the distinct "fourth join". https://studysites.uk.sagepub.com/sassoon/files/pupilpages/exit%20but%20not%20entry%20strokes.pdf · https://oak.quizalize.com/programmes/english-primary-ks1/units/the-four-joins-489
- **Argentina:** press snippet (Clarín/La Voz, full article not fetched) — experts defend cursive for fine motor and reading; its teaching is declining; Córdoba's curriculum prescribes no specific script. CABA Diseño Curricular / NAP text **not reached**: https://bde-ueicee.bue.edu.ar/documentos/482/download · https://progresionescaba.bue.edu.ar/fileprog/NP-PL-dise%c3%b1o-curricular.pdf

## 3. Game design for non-readers

- **Instructions:** spoken narration synced to animation; icons that suggest the gesture by themselves (secondary, not peer-reviewed). https://atendesigngroup.com/blog/young-audiences
- **Errorless vs productive failure:** guided/errorless first is better supported at this age; productive failure is inconclusive in young samples. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6435728/ · https://theconversation.com/failure-can-be-productive-for-teaching-children-maths-22418
- **Intrinsic integration (Habgood & Ainsworth, Zombie Division):** the mechanic must be the skill; the intrinsically integrated version was played ~7× longer; extrinsic stars/gates are the "chocolate-covered broccoli" pattern. https://www.de.ed.ac.uk/node/435 · https://giving.kqed.org/mindshift/20765/whats-the-secret-sauce-to-a-great-educational-game
- **Session length:** ~12–18 min on adult-directed tasks at 6, ~30 min upper bound (general guideline, not validated for touch games). https://www.earlyyears.tv/attention-span-development/
- **Adaptive difficulty:** no handwriting-specific evidence found (gap).

## 4. Per-screen checklist

1. Every instruction spoken + demonstrated; no reading required.
2. Icons alone suggest the gesture.
3. New stroke types follow the developmental order (line → circle → cross → diagonal → triangle).
4. Top→bottom, left→right by default; curved families start with the same curve consistently.
5. Trace with full guide first, then fade towards copying / memory.
6. Start point and direction marked or spoken before the attempt.
7. Full-finger targets, not pen-tip precision.
8. Corrective, neutral feedback; no fail state that stops progress.
9. The reward is the stroke itself (completion, character reaction), not an unrelated bonus.
10. Session of ~15–30 min with natural stopping points.
11. Letters taught by family, not alphabetically.
12. High-exit letters (b, o, v, w) get a distinct join cue.
13. Repetition with variation (unverified design inference).
14. The story frames each stroke as part of its goal (extrapolated from intrinsic integration).
15. Transfer to paper is provisionally supported for shape tracing, not verified for cursive letters.
